# Competitor study — kids' language and early-reading apps (2026-11-04, task #51)

Purpose: learn what makes successful kids' learning apps attractive, then turn it into original Little Roots tasks.
Rule: borrow principles, never characters, art, layouts or names. Voice rule: only the parent-recorded family voice (AI_DECISIONS 2026-11-04 "Family voice").

## Apps looked at
Duolingo ABC (reading, 3–7) · Khan Academy Kids (2–8) · Gus on the Go (vocabulary, many languages) · Lingokids ·
Endless Alphabet · Sago Mini / Toca Boca (open play). Sources are listed at the end; most are app pages, company
help pages and review sites, so treat them as patterns, not measured results.

## Rejected (and why)
- Speech grading and spoken narration (Duolingo ABC asks for the mic to grade the child): banned; the only voice is a
  grown-up's recordings (Family voice), and the microphone is asked only in the parent area.
- Account creation, "how did you hear about us" surveys, and notification prompts during onboarding: extra friction for parents and data the app doesn't need (PRIVACY.md).
- Paywall overlays on most screens: keep the paywall behind the parent gate only.
- Streak pressure aimed at the child: the streak stays a parent-facing stat; no "you lost your streak" messages.
- Constant floating-emoji rewards: Sago's restraint and the extrinsic-reward research (Lepper; Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999) suggest small, occasional rewards tied to finishing, not to every tap.
